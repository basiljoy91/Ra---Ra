# Ra & Ra BEYOND: independent commercial validation

Assessment date: **15 November 2026**. Primary market: Germany. Secondary scope: EU countries, with country-specific limitations.

**Investment conclusion: fund samples and a staged demand test; do not fund bulk production of all four designs on the available evidence.** BEYOND is a coherent creative direction, but neither the stories, the mockups, nor the wider market data establish that strangers will buy it at a profitable delivered price.

This is a desk assessment through seven professional lenses, not seven independently commissioned human experts or a consumer study. Recommendations, scores, persona reactions, budgets and gates are analytical judgments. No conversion rate, sales forecast or measured willingness to pay is claimed.

## 2. The four stories, assessed individually

### Collection introduction: BEYOND IS PERSONAL

The best line is **“We create the design. You give it meaning.”** It lets the wearer participate without claiming that a purchase changes their life. “One silhouette. Four expressions” is useful commercial organization.

The weakness is interchangeability. Fear, limits, reality and time could introduce a journal, fragrance, poster collection or wellness campaign. “Quiet, defining experiences that make us human” has emotional breadth but little brand specificity. There is no established creative provenance explaining why Ra & Ra owns this territory.

**Judgment:** credible as a collection framework; insufficient as a reason to pay a premium. Keep it short and connect it to visible design decisions. If there is a true founder/artist origin, tell that accurately; do not manufacture a dramatic biography.

### Beyond Fear

**Emotional appeal.** The unsent message, shaking voice and unfamiliar room are recognizable. Accepting that fear may remain is more humane than promising fearlessness. This is the most immediately understandable narrative and one of the two strongest emotionally.

**Generic elements.** The courage-versus-fear distinction is familiar self-help territory. The sequence of short rhetorical sentences becomes predictable. “That step can change an entire life” overstates what the preceding ordinary moment needs to mean. The line about refusing to remain still risks turning reassurance into pressure.

**Comprehension and representation.** A short explanation is accessible; the full English text adds reading effort. It can represent a first job, asking for help, relocation or a difficult conversation, but those are possible readings, not evidence that German customers identify with it.

**Memory, authenticity and tone.** “The step no one sees” is more distinctive than the general lesson about courage. The voice is polished and understandable, though still clearly advertising copy. It reads more premium when concrete and restrained, less so when explaining bravery at length. The German campaign line is intelligible but resembles a familiar motivational saying.

**Wearing and commercial appeal.** The small text and narrow graphic permit relatively private meaning. Someone could wear it without explaining the story. The story makes a simple line more legible conceptually, but offers no established reason to reject a better-fitting competitor.

**Without the brand:** the restrained artwork could still attract a minimalist buyer; the paragraph alone is not ownable. **Story is stronger than visual differentiation.** Improve and test the line as a distinctive graphic in its own right.

### Beyond Limits

**Emotional appeal.** Internalizing another person's definition of you is recognizable. Acknowledging real obstacles is a substantial improvement over “anything is possible.” People changing careers or returning to learning may find a point of connection.

**Generic elements.** “Too late / too difficult,” opportunities denied by the world and becoming someone new are well-used aspirational themes. This story is the longest relative to the simplicity of its graphic. It overlaps with Fear's territory of taking a first step, so the range does not yet offer four clearly distinct buying motives.

**Comprehension and representation.** The line continuing beyond a marker is understandable. “Not every line is yours to keep” is an awkward English headline: keeping a line is less natural than questioning an inherited label. Boundaries can also be protective; avoid suggesting that respecting limits is a personal failure. The German line about a boundary “belonging to you” remains abstract.

**Memory, authenticity and tone.** The solid-to-dotted change is more memorable than the title. The qualification about real obstacles lends credibility, while the eventual return to expanded possibilities weakens the restraint. It is the closest to conventional motivational merchandise.

**Wearing and commercial appeal.** The infinity symbol and “Beyond Limits” can suggest fitness, productivity or self-improvement. Some buyers will welcome that; others will avoid it. The story adds interpretation but does not eliminate the familiarity of the symbol.

**Without the brand:** easiest to mistake for generic print-on-demand merchandise. **Both narrative differentiation and artwork need improvement.** Keep the changing line; test removing the infinity symbol and one of the secondary placements.

There is also a specific design–story tension: the prose explicitly avoids claiming that everything is possible, while the line ends in an infinity symbol that can imply boundlessness. An unfinished dotted path would express uncertainty more consistently.

### Beyond Reality

**Emotional appeal.** Curiosity and reconsidering a first impression are thoughtful themes. However, the current story spends more time discussing perspective than depicting a moment a person can feel. It asks for intellectual agreement more than personal recognition.

**Generic elements.** Endings becoming beginnings and failures becoming new directions are familiar reframing devices. “Question an assumption” could belong to a corporate innovation campaign. The name may suggest escape or science fiction, while the story intends attentiveness within ordinary life.

**Comprehension and representation.** The eclipse metaphor works at the level of partial visibility. It does not automatically communicate empathy, listening or changed judgment. A customer can understand the picture and still miss the intended meaning. The German line about a new view changing the whole world is natural enough but grander than the concept requires.

**Memory, authenticity and tone.** “Look again. There is more” is concise and usable. The circular imagery is memorable; the explanatory essay is less so. It is appropriate for art-oriented fashion if presented as an invitation to interpretation, rather than a claim to philosophical depth.

**Wearing and commercial appeal.** The cosmic visual may sell to someone who has no interest in the narrative. Large back lettering and the diagram-like circle narrow its appeal among strict minimalists. The strongest use of story is to add a second reading after the customer likes the image.

**Without the brand:** visually plausible as a space-inspired graphic tee, but not obviously unique. **Design is stronger than the emotional story.** Build the rewrite around one ordinary change of perspective and reduce the back typography in a test variant.

### Beyond Time

**Emotional appeal.** An evening with friends, a familiar voice and an ordinary day gaining meaning later are the most emotionally specific material in the PDF. This is the strongest story for memory and attachment, with potential relevance beyond self-improvement.

**Generic elements.** “New chapters,” appreciating the present and a meaningful life measured by choices return to familiar inspirational language. The list of instructions—take the photo, make the call, enjoy the afternoon—risks telling the customer how to live. Memory should not become pressure or guilt.

**Comprehension and representation.** The experience is broad and recognizable. The title alone remains abstract. “Be here while it's here” is more ambiguous in English than the simpler closing thought. The German line about time passing and meaning remaining conveys the concept clearly, though it is not unique to this brand.

**Memory, authenticity and tone.** The ordinary evening is more convincing than universal claims about life. The story could feel personal without requiring a dramatic loss narrative. It is the most emotionally persuasive, provided the wearer is allowed to choose its meaning.

**Wearing and commercial appeal.** The detailed back graphic provides more visual content than Fear, but less versatility for a minimalist wardrobe. It could attract art/space-graphic buyers or be considered as a meaningful gift; neither use is validated.

**Without the brand:** the large graphic can attract attention, but celestial imagery is familiar. **The story has the broadest emotional appeal, while the artwork's reproduction is the largest technical uncertainty.** The PDF's “fading sequence of moons” does not accurately describe the supplied two-lunar-form composition. Correct this before testing.

## 3. Design–story compatibility and product execution

| Design | Observed visual and immediate reading | Compatibility and everyday wear | Main design action |
|---|---|---|---|

| Limits | Chest line crossing a marker into dots and an infinity sign; shoulder dots; small back infinity. | Most direct literal metaphor. | Reality | Eclipse-like chest motif; large thin circular diagram, axis and widely spaced back lettering. | Looks intentional and has thumbnail impact. Curiosity/perspective requires copy. | Time | Quiet serif chest lettering; two textured lunar forms, measured horizontal line, circular marks and dotted continuation on the back. | Strongest illustrative narrative surface, but visually busiest. Reads as astronomical/technical before memory or presence. Black keeps styling flexible; the back is not understated. | Correct the story description. **Collection-level weaknesses:** the common black base, spacious text and line/cosmic vocabulary create coherence, but this is not yet a unique visual system. Time uses a more editorial serif voice while the others lean toward technical sans-serif lettering. That can be intentional, but currently weakens immediate recognition as one maker. Consider a consistent hierarchy, label treatment and recurring small detail across all four, while preserving different central motifs.

The collection is restrained in colour, not uniformly minimal in graphic coverage. Calling every design “minimal” could attract buyers who dislike the actual back prints. Describe the product honestly: an oversized black tee with a restrained palette and individual graphic composition.

**Timelessness:** monochrome and simple geometry may age better than highly topical slogans, but oversized proportions, wide letterspacing and cosmic diagrams can still date. No design can be certified timeless from a mockup. Fear is least tied to a specific graphic genre; Reality and Time lean more strongly into space/technical aesthetics.

**Print-on-demand risk:** all four could be reproduced as generic graphic merchandise if sold with stock-like mockups, vague quality claims and no credible product evidence. Limits is most exposed; Fear can look underdesigned if the line feels incidental. Distinctive placement helps only if it is controlled, well executed and consistent across sizes.

**Required sample evidence:** front/back flat lays; daylight close-ups; garment measurements; movement video; multiple wearer heights and bodies; actual wash results; collar recovery; dimensional change; seam twist; pilling; black colour retention; print adhesion/handfeel. A heavier knit may feel structured to one person and warm or stiff to another. Ask testers rather than assuming weight equals comfort.

Supima's current system requires supply-chain participation, including the brand, through AQRe/TextileGenesis. A supplier assertion or old paper certificate is not sufficient authorization for a branded Supima claim. Verify the transaction chain and your use of the name before publishing “100% Supima.” [Supima licensing information, undated; accessed 15 November 2026](https://supima.com/industry-resources/license-information/).

**Quality versus storytelling:** the evidence for stated product priorities is stronger than the evidence for a narrative premium. It does not prove a precise causal ranking for this particular tee. The defensible working assumption is: show the design and garment first; test whether a concise story adds incremental buying intent.

### Scenario economics, not actual Ra & Ra margins

Model: VAT-registered domestic German sales at the standard **19% VAT** rate, with consumer prices inclusive of VAT. Small-business tax treatment, EU destination VAT, fulfillment country and import arrangements can change this. [Current German VAT Act §12, accessed 15 November 2026](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__12.html).

Assume one tee per retained order and landed garment costs of **€14 / €20 / €26**, including printing, labels, inbound freight and duty. These are sensitivity cases, not supplier quotations or evidence that certified Supima production is feasible at any one cost.

Other variable costs are assumed to be **€11.50 + 1.5% of consumer price**: packaging €1.25; pick/pack €2.50; outbound shipping subsidy €3.50; allocated return/defect burden €4; payment fee €0.25 + 1.5%. Costs exclude recoverable VAT where applicable. The €4 allocation is not a predicted return rate; it allocates failed-order costs to retained sales. Contribution is **before acquisition, fixed costs, founder pay and income/corporate tax**. It is not net profit. At €59 with €20 landed cost, product gross margin is approximately 59.7% of net sales, but the modeled contribution after other variable costs is only €17.19. A high product margin can therefore coexist with poor business economics.

`Contribution after acquisition = consumer price / 1.19 − landed cost − other variable costs − CAC`

`Break-even retained orders = fixed launch expense / contribution after acquisition`, rounded up, and only meaningful when the denominator is positive.

Example: €59 price, €20 landed cost, €10 CAC and €1,500 fixed launch expense imply about **€7.19 contribution and 209 retained orders** to recover that expense. That is arithmetic under assumed inputs, not a sales forecast. A 60-piece run would not recover those fixed expenses by itself. Unsold stock, payment timing and refund reserves add cash risk beyond contribution on sold units.

For an illustrative €5 contribution target before fixed expenses, allowable CAC in the base case is only **€3.94 at €49** and **€12.19 at €59**. Those are spending ceilings from the scenario, not attainable advertising costs. Estimate CAC using retained customers; early preorder CAC must be stress-tested for cancellations and returns.

Missing inputs: supplier quotes by quantity/size; fabric and print costs; setup/reject allowances; sampling; MOQ by design/size; freight/duty and origin; fulfillment; packaging; payment fees; shipping revenue; return/resale loss; photography/development expense; customer acquisition by channel; overhead; payment terms and lead times. Until these are known, a confident profitability verdict would be invented.

## 7. Limited editions and believable exclusivity

**Scarcity can intensify existing desire; it does not establish that the product is desired.** A 2023 meta-analysis of 37 articles and 335 effect sizes found heterogeneous scarcity effects, with demand-based scarcity stronger than restricted-supply cues. It does not estimate sales of unknown German apparel brands. [Ladeira et al., published 27 April 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.21816).

| Mechanism | Likely value for Ra & Ra now — judgment | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Numbered editions | Can make a piece feel personal; no demonstrated collector or resale value. Adds tracking and replacement complexity. | Optional only after choosing a real finite run. Define whether numbers apply per design or across the collection. |
| Small production batches | Credible operational fact and reduced stock exposure. Buyers may still not care about rarity. | Best starting approach: publish the actual first-run quantity and state whether restocks are possible. |
| Story card | Cheap way to connect the graphic to a private moment or gift. Some customers will discard it. | One simple card with a short line, care information and optional personal prompt. No elaborate manifesto. |
| Meaningful collection release | Builds coherence when artwork and real creative process justify the theme. | Explain the actual creative choices and show development/prototypes where truthful. |
| Collector packaging | Can inflate costs and shipping without improving the garment. Collectibility is unproven. | Defer rigid boxes and premium inserts. Prioritize fit, collar, print and real photography. |
| Seasonal drops | Can provide a reason to return, but create calendar and inventory pressure. | Release when the product is ready and demand supports it; avoid forced monthly scarcity. |
| Unique artwork/placement | Potentially improves the product itself and recognizability. | Highest-value differentiation if attractive and reproducible; test placement across sizes. |

Use **“First production run: [actual quantity]. Future restocks may follow”** if that is the business decision. Use a genuinely finite edition only if you will keep that promise. Never quietly reissue an allegedly never-repeated edition or reset a countdown. Fabricated limited-time claims intended to pressure immediate purchase are prohibited by the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. [Annex I(7), consolidated 27 September 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/2026-09-27/eng).

The cost-effective recommendation is a small truthful first batch, distinctive artwork, ordinary protective packaging and one good story/care card. Do not charge a collectible premium until buyers demonstrate that they value the edition.

## 10. Revised stories and commercial copy

These are proposed original drafts, not customer-tested claims or true accounts of the founder. They intentionally avoid promising personal transformation. Each product-page story below is approximately 80–120 words. Product specifications, verified provenance and fit information should sit in a separate factual block close to the buying controls.

### A shared collection website story

An unsent message. A description of yourself that no longer fits. A familiar place seen differently. An ordinary evening you still remember.

BEYOND begins with experiences like these. Each design gives one of them a visual form: a narrow opening, a line that changes, a partly hidden circle, a measure of passing time.

You may recognize something of your own in one of them. You may simply like the image. The meaning can stay private, and it can change as you do.

Four designs. One silhouette. Room for your own story.

### Beyond Fear

**Recommended change:** keep the ordinary beginning and the acceptance of uncertainty. Remove the life-changing promise and repeated explanations of bravery. Give the fine line a clear visual role without claiming that it literally glows.

**A. Website brand narrative**

Some beginnings are easy to miss from the outside. You send the message you have been rewriting. You enter a room where you know nobody. You say what you need, even though the words come slowly.

Beyond Fear begins in that small space between hesitation and action. A fine, wavering line opens through the dark, leaving most of what comes next unseen.

The design leaves room for the size of a step only you can know.

**B. Product-page story**

You write the message, read it once more, and finally press send. Nothing around you has changed. The room is quiet. You still do not know what comes next. But something you had been holding back is now outside your head.

Beyond Fear takes that ordinary moment as its starting point. A fine, wavering line opens through the dark, giving uncertainty a visible shape. It might remind you of an honest conversation, a new place, or an idea you finally shared. You decide which step it holds. There is no need for anyone else to know.

**C. Product hook:** For the step only you know the size of.

**D. Instagram caption:** The message sent. The first day. The conversation started. Beyond Fear.

**E. Personal connection:** You know what it took to begin.

### Beyond Limits

**Recommended change:** distinguish this from Fear by focusing on an inherited description becoming outdated. Avoid suggesting that health, financial or social barriers vanish through belief. Test a graphic without the terminal infinity symbol so the image supports an open path rather than boundlessness.

**A. Website brand narrative**

“I have never done that” can quietly become “I am not that kind of person.” A passing description settles into something that feels permanent.

Beyond Limits leaves room to question it. In the artwork, a solid line reaches a marker and continues as dots. The path becomes less certain, but there is still somewhere for it to go.

An old description can be part of your history without deciding what you try next.

**B. Product-page story**

You arrive at the class a few minutes early. Everyone seems to know where to stand. When someone asks whether you have done this before, you say no. For once, that answer leaves something open.

Beyond Limits is about the descriptions we collect and sometimes outgrow: too quiet, too late, not that kind of person. In the artwork, a solid line reaches a marker and continues as dots. The route becomes less certain. There is room for pauses, different directions and learning slowly. It is a small space for a version of yourself you have not met yet.

**C. Product hook:** An old description can leave room for someone new.

**D. Instagram caption:** First time in the room. Still finding your place. Beyond Limits.

**E. Personal connection:** Something you once ruled out may still be yours to try.

### Beyond Reality

**Recommended change:** choose perception as the one central idea. Drop the competing narratives of success, reinvention and hidden potential. Let the partial circle carry a simple relationship between what is visible and what remains outside the first view.

**A. Website brand narrative**

You hear the rest of a story and understand a familiar person differently. You return to a place and notice something that has always been there.

Beyond Reality begins with that shift in attention. Light catches the edge of a circle while most of it remains hidden. A first view can feel complete even when there is more outside it.

The design makes room for curiosity: about a place, another person, or an idea you have not finished considering.

**B. Product-page story**

You take the same route each morning. One day you slow down and notice a window, a tree, a small detail that has been there all along. The place has not changed, but your experience of it has.

Beyond Reality begins with that shift in attention. An eclipse-like edge reveals only part of a circle, leaving the rest to the eye and the imagination. The design can hold a second look at a familiar place, a conversation that changed an assumption, or a question you are still considering. There is room to look again before deciding what you see.

**C. Product hook:** A familiar world can still surprise you.

**D. Instagram caption:** Same route. Something you had never noticed. Beyond Reality.

**E. Personal connection:** Think of the conversation that changed your first impression.

### Beyond Time

**Recommended change:** retain the ordinary memory, remove instructions about living correctly, and describe the actual lunar forms and measured line. Let joy, gratitude and absence be possible readings without directing customers toward grief.

**A. Website brand narrative**

An ordinary evening becomes a photograph you return to. The room is familiar. Someone is caught halfway through a laugh. You remember staying longer than you had planned.

Beyond Time follows the distance between a passing moment and what it comes to mean. Lunar forms sit beside a measured line; the marks are even, but memories rarely are.

Some days leave very little behind. Others stay with us in ways we could not have known at the time.

**B. Product-page story**

You find an old photograph while looking for something else. There is a cup on the table, a familiar room, someone halfway through a laugh. You remember the day. At the time, it seemed ordinary.

Beyond Time holds that difference between how a moment passes and how it stays with us. Two lunar forms sit along a measured line, with marks continuing beyond them. Time can be counted; the meaning of an afternoon is harder to measure. The design leaves space for a person, a place, or a memory you carry into the days that follow.

**C. Product hook:** Some minutes stay for years.

**D. Instagram caption:** An ordinary evening. A photograph you keep coming back to. Beyond Time.

**E. Personal connection:** You have an ordinary day you would like to visit again.

Use the longer website narrative only where the visitor opts into it. On a product page, show real garment images, price, size, delivery and a short hook first. An expandable story can add depth without making reading a condition of understanding the product.

## 11. A low-budget demand-validation experiment

**Purpose:** identify a specific design, buyer group and economically viable offer before bulk production. This research should be allowed to reject the concept. Do not use it only to select the most flattering advertisement.

### Budget and sequence

Illustrative staged screening budget: **€1,480**, excluding founder time, bulk inventory, fulfillment, outside legal help and any additional adequately powered experiment. These are allowances, not quoted recruitment or manufacturing prices.

| Item | Allowance | What it buys / limitation |
|---|---:|---|
| Production-representative samples | €300 | First obtain quotes; may not cover all designs/sizes. Prioritize Time and Fear and a size spread. |
| 150 qualified German survey respondents | €450 | Assumes €3 per response/recruitment allowance. A commercial panel may cost more; adjust the plan rather than pretend the target was reached. |
| 12 interviews / fitting incentives | €180 | €15 each; remote/local mix. Separate compensation from buying or praising the product. |
| Instagram advertising | €500 | Exploratory creative and landing-page screening, not a promise of enough purchases for statistical conclusions. |
| Survey/landing tools | €50 | Use existing infrastructure where feasible. |
| **Total** | **€1,480** | Stop after a failed gate; do not spend all stages automatically. |

Indicative sequence is four to six weeks **after** suppliers can provide appropriate samples: setup and sourcing; survey/interviews; sample revisions; traffic pilot; then preorders only if ready. Supplier lead times may extend this. Avoid letting a calendar deadline force an unready launch.

### Experiment 1: unbranded design preference against real alternatives

Recruit **150 German adults** with a recent relevant purchase: an oversized or graphic tee in the last 12 months. Record actual brand, price and frequency of wear. Include people who reject inspirational clothing. Use balanced recruitment across ages 18–24, 25–34 and 35–45 and varied genders/body sizes; these are research quotas, not claims about market composition. Analyze the 20–34 core hypothesis separately without pretending small subgroups are representative.

Exclude friends, family and existing brand supporters from the cold-audience result; report them separately if included elsewhere. Do not screen for liking the BEYOND idea. Include a lower-spend subgroup to understand price rejection, but do not let unrelated bargain-only buyers determine the premium segment's product.

Show all four designs and **two matched competitor examples**, such as an understated tee and an art-led back-print tee. Use standardized front/back views with similar image quality, scale, background and garment proportions. Remove only brand cues that can fairly be removed; preserve the actual decorative design. If removing a logo destroys the comparator's design, choose a different product. Obtain appropriate rights for private research images and do not reuse competitor imagery in public ads.

Randomize order. Initially show **no brand, story, fabric claim or price**. Ask for first choice, second choice, “none,” everyday wearability, expected price, and what each image suggests. Make an additional unpriced paired design choice against the prespecified closest comparator, with “neither.” Then run the first price exposure exactly as specified in Experiment 3; do not show a common price beforehand. Reveal genuine product information and the story in separate later stages. Do not attribute a preference change solely to story if material, brand and story are revealed together.

**KPIs:** counts choosing each design; “none/neither”; selection against the closest comparator; spontaneous interpretation; willingness to wear in a named ordinary setting; change after price; recurring rejection reasons.

**Proposed management gate:** a finalist earns a physical/paid test if at least **60 of 150** choose it in the prespecified paired comparison over its comparator or neither, and at least **90 of 150** say they would realistically wear it in an ordinary setting. These deliberately directional thresholds are not industry benchmarks or evidence of market share. Forty percent in a three-option comparison does not mean the design defeated the established brand. Preselect the primary comparator before inspecting results.

If all candidates fail, revise the product before buying traffic. If one passes while most demand is confined to a small subgroup, test that subgroup rather than claiming broad German appeal. Report counts and uncertainty; convenience recruitment and repeat judgments prevent treating the results as a representative national poll.

### Experiment 2: 12 interviews plus physical fit and wear checks

Include approximately six likers and six rejecters, with women and varied bodies/heights represented. Where feasible, let at least eight handle and try the actual finalist samples. A physical comparator can reveal whether “premium” survives contact. Conceal brand labels for the first inspection where practical without altering garment feel.

Ask:

- “Tell me about the last T-shirt you paid for. What else did you consider?”
- “What, if anything, does this image suggest?”
- “Where would you wear this? Where would you avoid wearing it?”
- “What bothers you about the fit, print or wording?”
- “After reading this paragraph, what changed, if anything?”
- “At this total price, would you choose this, the other tee, or neither? What would you give up to buy it?”

Avoid “Does this inspiring story connect to your personal journey?” It tells the respondent the desired answer and presupposes a connection. Do not defend the art during the interview. Record exact objections before interpreting them.

**KPIs/gate:** resolve repeated severe fit/comfort/quality complaints. As a practical rule, if three or more independent wearers identify the same material problem, investigate it before proceeding; this is an engineering trigger, not a population estimate. Check a five-wash sample for visible deterioration and dimensions against a supplier-agreed specification. Five washes are an initial screen, not proof of lifetime durability. A story that is liked while the shirt remains unwanted is a failed product proposition.

### Experiment 3: exploratory price sensitivity

To keep the €450 survey allowance and sequence internally consistent, **prespecify Beyond Time as the exploratory price-test product before recruitment**. Within the same 150-person survey, randomly allocate **30 respondents each** to €39, €49, €59, €69 or €79. This must be each person's first displayed Ra & Ra price and their only Time price; complete the unpriced questions first. Show identical factual product evidence, label unverified specifications as proposed, and disclose whether shipping is included; compare the **total payable price**. Ensure allocation is balanced as far as possible by age and prior spending. Ask choice against actual alternatives at their real prices and “buy neither,” not only “Would you buy?”

Do not show everyone a five-step ascending ladder: the sequence anchors responses. Collect open-ended expected price before the assigned price. At only 30 per cell, results are noisy and should eliminate obviously weak options, not produce a precise demand curve. These findings apply only to Time. If Fear or another design wins the unpriced test, validate that finalist's price through a separately budgeted test or a real offer; do not transfer Time's price responses to it. A van Westendorp-style question can supplement perception research but would still be hypothetical.

**Gate:** eliminate any price that fails actual contribution economics, regardless of survey popularity. Select at most two economically feasible prices for subsequent real offers. Do not announce a winner from a handful of stated yeses. If customers only accept €39 and the actual economics require €59, the product/segment/cost proposition has failed this version of the test.

### Experiment 4: Instagram creative screening

Use two finalist designs × two approaches: **actual garment/artwork evidence** and **a short everyday story**. Keep format, audience, placement and offer comparable. Begin with roughly **€40 per creative (€160 total)** as a diagnostic allowance, not enough to establish sales performance. Use the platform's randomized split-test facility where available and record unequal delivery if it occurs.

Measure cost per qualified landing session, price-visible confirmed signup, checkout start and paid order, with likes/CTR as secondary attention indicators. Exclude bots and internal traffic. Tag campaign sources consistently. A high click rate with immediate price rejection is not a winning product ad.

**Gate:** advance no more than one or two creatives based on downstream behavior and credible interpretation. If counts are too small, mark the result inconclusive. Do not select the story version just because it attracts more reactions.

### Experiment 5: product-focused versus story-focused product pages

Use one finalist and one economically feasible price. Send the **same incoming creative** to randomly assigned pages; hold photographs, factual specifications, price, delivery, returns and purchase controls constant. Vary one defined message area between product-focused and story-focused copy of comparable prominence. Both variants retain essential product information. This isolates the messaging package more cleanly than comparing two entirely different sites.

The remaining **€340 advertising allowance** can begin the pilot, with eligible non-friend organic traffic analyzed separately. Aim for at least **150 valid sessions per arm** as an exploratory minimum, but do not assume that budget will obtain them. Before money can legally and operationally be accepted, the primary KPI is a confirmed, price-visible, design/size-specific early-access signup. Once ready, use paid preorder and later retained contribution as the primary commercial outcome.

**Limits:** 150 sessions per arm cannot reliably detect modest purchase changes. As a statistical illustration, detecting a change from 2% to 4% at conventional 5% significance and 80% power requires roughly **1,100–1,200 sessions per arm**. Those percentages are invented for the power example, not expected conversion. A small pilot can reveal major failures but often cannot establish a story effect.

**Gate:** do not call a winner from one or two extra orders or from signup lift alone. Predefine the practical improvement worth acting on, confidence method and maximum sample/spend. If evidence is insufficient, retain the clearer, lower-friction page and label the story effect unproven. If story increases signups but not paid orders or contribution, it has not validated a commercial premium.

### Experiment 6: price-visible waitlist, then genuine paid preorder

An early-access page should show the **intended price, shipping charge or included delivery, expected timing, actual sample photos, design and size choice**. Confirm email addresses; separate marketing consent from order/operational messages. Count unique confirmed people. Do not imply a product is available for immediate shipment if it is only a concept.

Then, after samples and supply are ready, offer a real preorder at the actual economically viable price. A refundable reservation can be an intermediate step, but its low commitment makes it weaker evidence than paying full price. Checkout-intent research must clearly state that no purchase is occurring; do not simulate successful charges or make a fake sold-out claim.

**Proposed directional paid-demand gate:** at least **30 unrelated full-price buyers**, recorded by source, after immediate cancellations. This is a practical minimum signal, not statistical proof of scale or permission to order a much larger run. The screening budget may not obtain 30 buyers; failing to reach the sample within the cap is a limitation, not a reason to fabricate confidence or keep spending indefinitely.

**Manufacturing gate:** proceed only when verified paid commitments and explicitly budgeted inventory risk support the actual supplier MOQ, selected design and size distribution. Prefer a run substantially covered by commitments plus a small, funded exchange allowance. Thirty purchases do not justify 300 units. If MOQ and commitments do not match, negotiate a smaller run or hold and refund according to the stated terms.

Compute acquisition cost separately for cold paid customers and existing/organic audiences. Require positive contribution under a conservative cancellation/return scenario and enough remaining margin to recover fixed costs. In the illustrative €59 base model with a €5 contribution target, **€12.19 is the CAC ceiling**; use the ceiling calculated from real costs instead. Do not assume paid traffic becomes cheaper as spending grows.

### Preorder readiness in Germany, as of this assessment date

This is the operational minimum relevant to the experiment, not a complete legal certification of the business:

- State the seller identity/contact, total price, shipping costs, realistic agreed delivery period, production trigger, withdrawal information and refund procedure before accepting payment.
- Ordinary online goods normally carry a **14-day withdrawal period from delivery**. A standard-size tee, routine preorder or numbered edition is not automatically customized under the exception. [Your Europe returns, checked 28 April 2026](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/returns/index_en.htm); [BGB §312g](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312g.html).
- Delivery is normally within **30 days unless a different period is specifically agreed**. Put a realistic preorder delivery agreement in place rather than using indefinite “shipping soon.” [Your Europe delivery, checked 17 August 2026](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/shipping-delivery/index_en.htm).
- Since **19 June 2026**, German consumer distance contracts concluded through an online interface and subject to a statutory withdrawal right require an accessible electronic withdrawal function during that period, with confirmation and durable acknowledgment. Standard BEYOND preorders would ordinarily be covered. A contact email or return-policy page alone does not implement it. [Federal Government announcement, 19 June 2026](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/bundesregierung/bundeskanzleramt/besserer-verbraucherschutz-2382536); [current BGB §356a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__356a.html).
- Keep cash available for VAT, refunds and fulfillment. Preorders are obligations to customers; they are not free financing or final retained sales. Confirm applicable product, labeling, privacy and seller obligations before opening the real payment flow.

### Strength of signals and stopping rules

**Likes < clicks < confirmed signup after seeing price < size-selected checkout start < refundable deposit < full-price paid preorder < delivered and retained purchase < repeat purchase.** Each step answers a different question. Even preorders can cancel or return.

Before recruiting, write down the main KPI, sample target, target audience, comparator, time/spend cap, exclusions and decision rules. Keep all respondents in the assigned test group where appropriate; do not discard negative answers or stop on a lucky day. Report absolute counts, not only percentages. Do not extrapolate small subgroup findings to Germany or the EU.

If a real, functioning, fairly priced purchase test receives zero orders, investigate measurement, page function, delivery promise and product fit before interpreting it. For context, zero events in 300 independent comparable visits gives a rough 95% upper rate bound of about 1% under a simple binomial model; visits are not always independent or representative. This can be compared with the conversion economics require, but is not a universal “bad product” threshold.

1. **First manufacturing batch:** provisionally Beyond Time, only after the tests; switch to Fear if customer evidence favors it. Do not let this desk ranking override paid preferences or size-level commitments.
2. **Emotionally strongest story:** Beyond Time. Fear is the clearest everyday emotional premise and a close second.
3. **Most promising commercial positioning:** Time as an art-led black oversized tee with private emotional meaning; Fear as the more broadly wearable restrained alternative. These are different segments and should be tested as such.
4. **What buyers would be paying for:** primarily the combination of aesthetics, fit, fabric feel and dependable execution; story may personalize the choice. Status and collectibility are currently unearned. Exclusivity should be an honest production fact, not the central price justification.

It is differentiated enough to justify testing a collection, but generic titles, common symbols and universal life themes are easy to imitate. A lasting proposition needs a recognizable design language, consistent garment quality, truthful creative provenance and customers who return after wearing it.
6. **What must change before launch:** obtain actual samples and Supima authorization/provenance; select a behavioral target audience; correct visual/copy mismatches; shorten the stories; prove fit on relevant bodies; verify costs and fulfillment; complete purchase/withdrawal functions; replace critical mockups with real product evidence; choose the first design from demand rather than founder preference.
7. **Three strongest experiments:** a full-price preorder with strangers at a viable delivered price; a blind physical/design comparison against real alternatives; and a randomized product-versus-story page test judged on paid behavior and contribution. Surveys support these experiments but cannot replace them.
8. **What justifies investment:** production-ready samples that withstand wear/wash checks; a finalist preferred enough against real substitutes; unrelated buyers paying the needed price; acquisition and retained-order contribution within the actual financial ceiling; commitments supporting MOQ and size mix; and sufficient cash to fulfill/refund. **What argues against launch:** enthusiasm only from friends; high story approval with low willingness to wear; sales only at an uneconomic discount; sample failures; unverifiable quality claims; excessive acquisition cost; or demand too small for the required batch.

**If I were investing my own money into Ra & Ra, would I fund the first BEYOND collection based on the available evidence?**

**I would fund a capped sample-and-validation stage, not the four-design manufacturing launch.** The evidence supports a plausible product category and two worthwhile candidates, Time and Fear. It does not establish preference at a profitable price, reliable product execution, achievable acquisition cost or sufficient volume. Those are the risks that turn a good story into unsold stock. I would release manufacturing money only after real customers, real garments and real unit economics support the decision.
